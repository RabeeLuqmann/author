Featured novel

The Wolf and the Gazelle

A literary thriller of survival and trust

“He was a wolf surviving in the shadows. She was a gazelle fleeing a gilded cage. Together, they must outrun the hunters.”

Sami is a red-haired Jordanian labourer, hardened by loss. After intervening when a woman is subjected to police brutality, he is captured, tortured and forced to flee Jordan with little more than a forged passport and the will to survive.

Farrah, a princess from Dubai escaping a forced marriage, is on the run too. In Albania, Sami finds her among the displaced, with mercenaries closing in. Their dangerous journey through the Balkans becomes a story of survival, trust and two wounded people finding strength together.

A little about me

Across cultures.
Into the human heart.

“Every story begins with a human truth.”

Rabeè Luqmann is a German-Jordanian novelist whose work explores love, desire, freedom and the human condition across cultures. His stories move between different societies and intimate worlds, asking how people navigate the forces that shape their lives.

From the tensions between tradition and personal choice in Bound by Destiny, to the complicated emotional landscape of Endorphin: The Ache of Wanting, Luqmann's novels examine the choices people make when longing, loyalty and expectation collide.

The Daughter Who Was Not Named turns to a story inspired by real events, while The Wolf and the Gazelle explores survival, trust and the pursuit of freedom across borders.
